\section{Other Programs of Note}
There are two other pieces of software that also should be mentioned.
Pulled\_Pork and Oinkmaster are both programs that can download rules and update
your Snort sensor, and even do some of the more advanced features you
will find in Snowman and BHTB. Pulled\_Pork even has support for the Shared
Object rules\cite{SnortManual}, something this project has not taken into
account due to complexity. 

However, both of these programs suffer from the fact that they are
command line oriented programs. They are both very good at what they do, but do not have the userability and
efficiency that you get from a GUI with buttons. In a limited environment with
one or very few Snort sensors, they are probably a better choice for rule
management than something like BHTB or Snowman. But once the environment grows
larger, with tens or even hundreds of sensors, they would start to slow the user
down.

\section{Research on Software Performance}
Since this project also investigates software performance, it is relevant to
take a look at what others in the field write on the subject. We have noticed
that most development reports from GUC do not really mention the subject,
probably due to most student projects being more or less agile\cite{AGILE} development projects.
This is something mentioned in \cite{FutureEngineering}, that agile programming
is becoming mainstream and by it's nature is a concern for the future of software
performance engineering, as the primary focus of an agile project is to pump out
functionality.

Early on in the project we noted that Snowman is very I/O bound, as it does a
lot of database operations. So much so that there is a very noticable difference
in performance between traditional rotational harddrives and SSD,
as seen in section \ref{tester}.

Myers speaks of this in his Master thesis\cite{Myers}. His tests show that SSD
has a significant advantage over HDD when it comes to random read performance
but not when it comes to write performance. This is to be expected as his tests
were conducted in 2008 and SSD technology was fairly new back then, suffering from
their different way of writing data\footnote{Features such as TRIM was
introduced shortly after Myers thesis.}. As operating systems, drivers and disk
controllers has improved, SSD's are now superior to HDD's in most tasks.

When we identified software performance testing as one of the goals of our
project, we needed to identify a good testing method. Sadly, there is not a
whole lot written on the subject, assuming most testing just follows the
scientific method or common sense. However, the articles \cite{WOSP98} and
\cite{WOSP04} gave us a few pointers towards developing the test cases described
in chapter \ref{relevant kapittel}. Both articles also stress the importance of
performance testing during the development rather than after it, as it gives you
time to actually implement optimizations and improvements.
